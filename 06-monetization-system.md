# 06 — Complete Monetization System

*YouTube Partner Program preparation, AdSense strategy, affiliate marketing, sponsorships, digital products, courses, memberships, newsletters, communities, and other revenue streams — sequenced by channel growth stage.*

## 1. YouTube Partner Program (YPP) preparation

**Eligibility (verify current thresholds on YouTube's official Partner Program page before applying, as requirements can change):** historically, either (a) 500 subscribers + 3,000 valid public watch hours in the trailing 12 months + 3 uploads in the last 90 days, or (b) 500 subscribers + 3M valid public Shorts views in the trailing 90 days.

**Prep checklist (do these well before you hit the threshold, not after):**
- [ ] AdSense account created and linked, tax/payment info completed in advance so approval isn't delayed by paperwork
- [ ] Channel has zero unresolved Community Guidelines strikes
- [ ] Content is consistently advertiser-friendly (check the yellow-icon monetization status on recent uploads as a leading indicator)
- [ ] Two-step verification enabled on the channel (required for YPP)
- [ ] A content library (per file `01`/`02`) that's clearly one identifiable channel, not a scattershot of unrelated content

**Apply the moment you cross the threshold** — there's no benefit to waiting, and review can take days to weeks.

## 2. Revenue streams by channel growth stage

The core principle: **most of these don't require YPP and shouldn't wait for it.** Introduce them as soon as they're credible, not as soon as they're "allowed."

### Stage 0 — Pre-launch / 0-100 subscribers
- **Goal:** build the asset, not the income.
- **Revenue to introduce:** none, aside from passive links (Bandcamp/streaming links in every description) — no direct asks yet, audience is too small for conversion to matter more than growth.

### Stage 1 — Early traction / 100-500 subscribers
- **Direct fan funding:** "name your price" or pay-what-you-want digital downloads (Bandcamp or similar) for finished songs/stems — works at any audience size because it's fan-initiated, not ad-based.
- **Affiliate marketing (light touch):** honest affiliate links for the specific gear/plugins/software actually used and shown in videos (disclosed per FTC/YouTube guidelines) — low-effort, credible at this size because it's tied to real, shown usage.
- **Community building:** start a free Discord/community space now, while it's small enough to personally engage every member — this becomes the seed of paid memberships later.

### Stage 2 — YPP-eligible / 500+ subscribers, 3,000 watch hours
- **AdSense (ads on videos):** turn on immediately upon approval. At this size, ad revenue is a modest supplement, not a living — treat it as validation the channel is real, not yet a strategy.
- **Channel Memberships / Super Thanks:** enable both — even a handful of members at this stage funds gear upgrades and validates that some fans will pay directly.
- **First sponsorships:** music-software/plugin/gear brands regularly sponsor small creators (500-5,000 subs) at modest flat fees or affiliate-plus-fee deals, specifically because of high audience trust at this size — pitch brands you already use, don't wait to be approached.

### Stage 3 — Scaling / 1,000-10,000 subscribers
- **Digital products:** sell your own sample packs, presets, or stems from your production process — a natural, low-cost-to-produce product line directly demonstrated in your existing content.
- **Paid tutorials/mini-courses:** package your most-requested technique content (from file `07`'s "what worked" data) into a paid, deeper version — free YouTube content is the funnel, the paid course is the deeper product for your most invested fans.
- **Newsletter:** start an email list now (even a simple one) — it's the one audience channel you fully own, insulated from algorithm changes, and becomes critical for launching future products/releases directly.
- **Paid community tier:** convert the free Discord from Stage 1 into a tiered membership (free + paid tier with extra access, feedback, early releases).
- **Sync licensing:** submit finished instrumental/production work to sync libraries and licensing platforms — passive, compounding revenue that doesn't require additional audience size.

### Stage 4 — Established / 10,000+ subscribers
- **Larger sponsorships and brand deals:** now negotiating from a position of proven, consistent performance data (file `07`'s historical numbers are your leverage).
- **Full courses:** a structured, comprehensive paid course on your specific process/niche angle, sold to the newsletter + community built in Stage 3.
- **Merchandise:** physical products tied to the band/artist identity — credible once there's a real fanbase, not before.
- **Live shows/events:** the channel becomes a legitimate audience-building tool for real-world performance revenue.

## 3. Sequencing principle

Each stage's revenue streams are additive, not replacements — a Stage 4 channel still runs AdSense, affiliate links, memberships, *and* the newer streams. The mistake to avoid is either (a) trying to monetize everything at once before the audience can support it (kills trust and conversion), or (b) waiting for YPP/a big milestone to make any money at all (leaves real revenue on the table for months or years unnecessarily).

## 4. What to track per revenue stream (feeds into file `07`)

| Stream | Key metric |
|---|---|
| AdSense | RPM/CPM, total ad revenue |
| Memberships/Super Thanks | Active members, monthly recurring revenue |
| Sponsorships | Deals closed, average deal size, rate-per-1000-views benchmark |
| Digital products | Units sold, conversion rate from video views |
| Affiliate | Click-through and conversion rate per link |
| Newsletter | List size, open rate (health of your owned audience) |
| Sync licensing | Submissions made, placements landed |

Review this table monthly alongside the content metrics in file `07` — revenue and content performance should be reviewed together, since the content is what drives every stream above it.
