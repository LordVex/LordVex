# 01 — Channel Operating System

*Positioning, content pillars, upload strategy, formats, growth systems, retention framework, monetization plan, and weekly execution schedule.*

## 1. Channel positioning statement

> **Krymzia is the channel where a solo/independent artist builds a project in public — the songs, the production decisions behind them, and the reality of doing it without a label.**

Positioning has three parts, and all three must be true simultaneously or the channel is generic:

- **Who it's for:** aspiring/hobbyist musicians and producers who make music alone, plus a fan layer that just wants the songs and the story.
- **What it delivers that a similar channel doesn't:** most artist channels either (a) only post music videos with zero process, or (b) only post tutorials with no real artistic output. Krymzia does both from one identity — real songs get released *and* the decisions behind them get shown.
- **The emotional promise:** "you're watching someone actually build something, not perform expertise." That's the differentiator against polished major-label content and against faceless tutorial channels.

Write this statement into your channel's About section, almost verbatim. Every video idea gets tested against it: *does this serve the "building in public" promise?* If not, it's a different channel — Shorts-only side channel, personal vlog, whatever — not this one.

## 2. Content pillars

Pick 3–4 pillars max. More than that and the algorithm and the audience both lose the ability to predict what you upload, which kills suggested-video traffic (YouTube recommends channels it can pattern-match).

| Pillar | % of uploads | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Original Music** (music videos, lyric videos, performance videos) | 30% | The actual product; what fans subscribe for long-term | "Krymzia — [Song Title] (Official Video)" |
| **Process/Behind-the-Music** | 35% | The differentiator; highest suggested/search potential | "I wrote and produced a song in 48 hours — here's every decision" |
| **Artist Reality/Vlog** | 20% | Parasocial connection, retention driver, Shorts source | "What nobody tells you about releasing music with 200 subscribers" |
| **Skill/Tutorial (production, songwriting, gear)** | 15% | Search traffic, evergreen watch-hours | "The 3-chord trick I use in every Krymzia song" |

Rule: never let "Original Music" alone carry the channel early on. Pure music videos have the worst discovery economics on YouTube for unknown artists (low search volume for your name, no watch-time depth, no clear thumbnail/title hook). Process and reality content is what gets you found; music videos are what convert that audience into fans.

## 3. Video formats (by pillar)

- **Long-form process documentaries** (8–15 min): "making of" a specific track, start to finish. This is your flagship discovery format — it has narrative structure, a title/thumbnail hook, and search intent ("how to make a song from scratch").
- **Short-form vertical (Shorts, 15–60s):** hook-driven cuts from process videos, "wait for it" production moments, 15-second song previews, reaction-bait ("rating my own old songs"). This is your volume/reach engine.
- **Official music videos/visualizers** (3–5 min): the product itself, released on a schedule tied to actual songs.
- **Livestreams** (optional, once you have 50+ subs): writing/producing live. Cheap to produce, strong for real-time community and for YouTube's "watch time per session" signal.
- **Tutorials/breakdowns** (5–10 min): a specific reusable technique, evergreen and search-optimized.

## 4. Upload strategy

- **Minimum viable cadence: 1 long-form + 3 Shorts per week**, sustained for 12+ months. Consistency beats intensity — a channel that never misses a week beats one that posts 5 videos then goes dark for a month, because YouTube's recommendation system needs a stable pattern to learn who to show you to.
- **Best days:** publish long-form on the same 1–2 days every week (pick based on when your specific audience is active — start with Thu/Fri evening or weekend mornings, then let Analytics correct you after 8 weeks of data). Shorts can go out any day; treat them as always-on.
- **Batch production, staggered release:** film/produce in batches (see file `05`) so you always have 2–3 weeks of buffer. Never publish same-day-as-filmed as your default mode — one sick week or one bad take shouldn't break the schedule.
- **Release a song → immediately produce 3–5 pieces of process content from that single release cycle** (making-of, lyric breakdown, "reacting to the final mix," a Short of the hardest part). One song = one content cycle, not one video.

## 5. Growth systems

1. **Suggested-traffic loop:** every video should be clearly "more of the same" as your last 5 videos in topic and thumbnail style, so YouTube can confidently recommend it to people who watched your other videos. Novelty in *content*, consistency in *format/packaging*.
2. **Search-traffic loop:** at least one upload per month directly targets a keyword with real search volume ("how to write a chorus," "lo-fi production tutorial," "how to release music independently"). Check volume in YouTube's search-suggest and in Analytics > Research.
3. **Shorts-to-long funnel:** every Short ends with a spoken/text pointer to the long-form video it's cut from ("full breakdown linked/in my videos"). Shorts win reach; long-form wins watch-hours and subscriber depth — you need both, and Shorts should feed long-form, not compete with it.
4. **Collaboration loop:** once you have ~20 solid videos, guest on / feature other small artist-producer channels in your subscriber range (500–5,000). Cross-audience exposure is the fastest external growth lever pre-monetization, and it costs nothing but outreach.
5. **Community loop:** Community tab posts (polls on next song direction, behind-the-scenes photos) between uploads keep the channel active in subscribers' feeds without production cost, and available once you hit 500 subs.

## 6. Retention framework

- **First 15 seconds:** state the promise of the video out loud or on-screen ("today I'm turning this voice memo into a finished song") — no logo intro, no "hey guys welcome back."
- **Pattern interrupt every 30–45 seconds:** a cut, a new visual, a text overlay, a question to camera. Flat single-shot talking segments longer than 45s are the #1 retention killer in this niche.
- **Open loops:** tease the payoff early ("the final version at the end sounds nothing like this" ) and don't resolve it until 70–80% through the video.
- **Retention graph triage (weekly):** pull up Audience Retention for your latest video. A steep drop in the first 30s = hook/thumbnail mismatch problem. A slow steady bleed throughout = pacing problem. A spike (rewatch) = find what caused it and do more of it.

## 7. Monetization plan (summary — full detail in file `06`)

- **Pre-YPP (0–500 subs):** build the audience asset; sell nothing except maybe a Bandcamp/merch link in description. Focus 100% on watch hours and subscriber growth.
- **At YPP eligibility (500 subs + 3,000 watch hours/12mo, OR 3M Shorts views/90 days):** apply immediately. Turn on ads.
- **Parallel/early revenue (works even pre-YPP):** direct fan funding (Bandcamp, Patreon-style memberships, "name your price" downloads), sync licensing submissions, affiliate links for gear/plugins you actually use.
- **Post-YPP, scaling (1,000+ subs):** channel memberships, Super Thanks, sponsorships from music-software/gear brands, paid production tutorials/courses, licensing your beats/stems.

## 8. Weekly execution schedule (template)

| Day | Task |
|---|---|
| **Mon** | Weekly analytics review (file `07`). Pick this week's long-form topic from the idea bank (file `02`). |
| **Tue** | Write/outline long-form script + record voiceover/A-roll. |
| **Wed** | Film/produce remaining footage + music session work. |
| **Thu** | Edit long-form video. Cut 2–3 Shorts from this week's + last week's footage. |
| **Fri** | Publish long-form (afternoon/evening). Design thumbnail, write title using file `03`'s checklist. Schedule Shorts for Sat/Sun/Mon. |
| **Sat** | Community post. Engage with every comment on the new video within first 24h (strong early-engagement signal). Outreach to 1 potential collab channel. |
| **Sun** | Light day: batch-plan next week's topics, restock idea bank, rest. |

Adjust days to your real life, but keep the *sequence* (review → plan → make → edit → publish → engage → replenish) — that sequence is what prevents the two most common failure modes: publishing with no plan, and planning with no review.
