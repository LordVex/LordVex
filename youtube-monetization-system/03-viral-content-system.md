# 03 — Viral Scripts, Hooks & Thumbnails System

*A repeatable system for high-CTR, high-retention videos: concepts, hooks, titles, thumbnails, opening scenes, storytelling structure, retention techniques, CTAs, and full scripts.*

## 1. The core principle

Every video succeeds or fails on **three decisions**, made in this order, before a single frame is shot:

1. **The concept** — is there an inherent hook here, or are you hoping editing will save a flat idea?
2. **The title + thumbnail pair** — will this win the click against everything else in the feed?
3. **The first 30 seconds** — will this keep the click from bouncing?

Everything after that is execution. A great edit cannot save a boring concept; a boring thumbnail can kill a great video before anyone sees the edit.

## 2. Concept-testing checklist

Before scripting, run every idea through these questions:

- **Curiosity gap:** does the title/concept create a question the viewer needs answered? ("I turned a voice memo into a song" implies "how?" and "does it sound good?")
- **Stakes:** is there something to win or lose? (a deadline, a bet, a risk of failure, money, reputation)
- **Specificity:** "How I Write Songs" is vague; "I Wrote a Song Using Only 3 Sounds" is specific and visual.
- **Relatability or aspiration:** does the *persona* (file `02`) see themselves in this, or see who they want to become?

If an idea scores low on all four, it's a Community post or a Short, not a long-form video.

## 3. Hooks (first 3 seconds — spoken/visual, before any intro)

Use these hook templates, rotate so none becomes stale:

- **The stakes-first hook:** "If this song doesn't get 1,000 plays, I'm scrapping the whole album." *(state the risk immediately)*
- **The result-first hook:** show/play the finished result for 2 seconds, then cut to "here's how that happened." *(open loop — resolve at the end)*
- **The contradiction hook:** "Everyone says you need expensive gear to sound professional. I made this with $40 of software." *(challenges an assumption)*
- **The confession hook:** "I almost didn't post this because it exposes how bad my first mixes were." *(vulnerability = curiosity)*
- **The direct-address hook:** "If you've ever had a voice memo you were too scared to finish — this is for you." *(speaks straight to persona)*
- **The countdown/constraint hook:** "I have 48 hours to finish this song before [deadline]." *(built-in urgency and structure)*

**Never** open with a logo animation, "hey guys, welcome back to my channel," or a thank-you-for-subscribing line. Every second before the hook is a second of retention lost.

## 4. Titles

**Formula bank** (pick one that fits the concept honestly — never bait a payoff the video doesn't deliver):

- `I [did specific thing] in [timeframe/constraint]` → "I Wrote a Song in 48 Hours With No Inspiration"
- `The [surprising number/fact] no one tells you about [topic]` → "The Real Streaming Payout at 10,000 Plays"
- `[Do X] with only [constraint]` → "Mixing a Vocal With Zero Expensive Gear"
- `I tried [thing], here's what happened` → "I Let AI Write My Lyrics — Here's What Happened"
- `Why [common belief] is wrong` → "Why 'You Need Studio Gear' Is a Myth"
- `[Number]-word/step guide to [outcome]` → "The 3-Chord Trick Behind Every Hit Chorus"

**Title rules:**
- Front-load the specific, curiosity-driving words in the first 40 characters (mobile truncation).
- One clear promise per title — don't stack two hooks.
- Use numbers and concrete nouns over adjectives ("48 hours" beats "quickly"; "$40" beats "cheap").
- A/B feel-test by reading it aloud: would a stranger scrolling stop on this?

## 5. Thumbnails

**The 3-second rule:** a thumbnail must communicate its idea before it's even in focus, at phone-scroll size.

- **One focal point.** One face (strong emotion — surprise, focus, frustration — not a neutral smile) or one object (the gear, the song title card, a visual of the constraint).
- **3–5 words max of text**, high-contrast, large enough to read at thumbnail size on a phone. The text should add information the image alone doesn't give (e.g., image = shocked face at mixing desk, text = "IT ALMOST DIDN'T WORK").
- **Consistent template per content category** (file `02`'s six categories) — same font, same color-grade treatment, same badge/logo placement — so the channel is instantly recognizable in a subscription feed while each individual thumbnail still varies by content.
- **Contrast against YouTube's white/dark UI:** avoid pure white backgrounds; saturated, non-skin-tone colors pop hardest in-feed.
- Always design thumbnail **and** title together, as a pair — the thumbnail shows, the title tells; if both say the same thing, you've wasted one of them.

## 6. Opening scenes (first 15–30 seconds after the hook)

1. Hook (0:00–0:03, above)
2. **Promise statement** (0:03–0:10): plainly state what the viewer gets — "By the end of this, you'll hear the full song and see every mistake I made getting there."
3. **Stakes/context** (0:10–0:20): why does this matter right now (deadline, personal stakes, audience request)?
4. **Roadmap cut** (0:20–0:30, optional for longer videos): a fast flash-forward montage of key moments to come — this creates multiple open loops at once.

Only after this does any "channel intro" material belong — and it should be near-instant (a 1-second animated bumper, not a spoken introduction).

## 7. Storytelling structure (long-form)

Use a three-act structure mapped to a real production/creative journey — it's already naturally dramatic, don't force artificial drama on top of it:

- **Act 1 — Setup (first ~20%):** the goal, the constraint/stakes, starting point (rough voice memo, blank page, broken gear).
- **Act 2 — Struggle (middle ~60%):** real obstacles as they happen (a part that isn't working, a decision between two directions, self-doubt). Include at least one moment that looks like it might fail — genuine tension outperforms manufactured positivity.
- **Act 3 — Resolution (final ~20%):** the payoff (finished song, finished mix, lesson learned), delivered in full, followed immediately by the CTA.

## 8. Retention techniques (apply throughout, not just at the open)

- **Pattern interrupts every 30–45 seconds:** new camera angle, cutaway, text overlay, sound effect, or scene change.
- **Verbal/visual chaptering:** "okay, step two" or on-screen chapter cards reset attention and let YouTube's chapter markers help suggested/search placement.
- **Micro-cliffhangers:** end segments mid-thought ("...and that's when I realized the whole chorus was wrong — but first, let me show you why the verse worked") to bridge into the next section.
- **Payoff pacing:** never resolve the central open loop (from the hook) before ~70% mark. If it resolves early, the remaining runtime has nothing to pull viewers through.
- **Audio hygiene:** consistent loudness, no dead air longer than ~1 second, music bed under process footage — silence and dead air are retention killers, especially against loud recommended-feed content.

## 9. CTAs (calls to action)

- **One CTA per video, placed at peak engagement — right after the payoff, not at the start and not scattered throughout.**
- Match the CTA to the video's natural next step: a making-of video → "the finished song is out now, link in the description"; a tutorial → "try this on your own track and tag me"; a reality/vlog video → "subscribe if you want to see this project actually happen."
- Subscribe asks work best when tied to a *specific reason* ("subscribe to see if this song hits 1,000 plays") rather than a generic "please subscribe."
- Never place a CTA before the hook resolves — it reads as an interruption, not an invitation.

## 10. Full script skeleton (copy this per video)

```
[HOOK — 0:00-0:03]
(spoken line or visual moment that creates the curiosity gap)

[PROMISE — 0:03-0:10]
"By the end of this video, you'll [specific outcome]."

[STAKES/CONTEXT — 0:10-0:20]
Why this matters right now. What's on the line.

[ACT 1 — SETUP]
Starting point. The constraint. What "success" looks like.

[ACT 2 — STRUGGLE] (longest section — include real obstacles)
Beat 1: first attempt / first decision
Beat 2: what goes wrong or gets harder
Beat 3: the turn — what changes, what's learned
(pattern interrupt every 30-45s; at least one micro-cliffhanger)

[ACT 3 — RESOLUTION]
The full payoff, delivered clearly (play the song, show the result, state the lesson).

[CTA — final 15-20s]
One clear, specific ask tied directly to what just happened.
```

## 11. Why each element works (the "why" behind the system)

- Hooks and thumbnails win the **click** (Impressions → CTR) by exploiting curiosity gaps and pattern recognition at a glance.
- Promise + stakes statements reduce early **bounce** by telling the algorithm's real judge — the viewer — that their time investment will pay off.
- Pattern interrupts and micro-cliffhangers fight the natural attention decay curve every video faces, directly improving **Average View Duration** and **Audience Retention %**.
- A single, well-placed CTA converts already-engaged attention into the actions (subscribe, watch next, comment) that compound into suggested-video reach — see file `04` for exactly how those actions feed the algorithm.
