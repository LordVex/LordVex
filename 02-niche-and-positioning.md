# 02 — Niche, Positioning & First 100 Video Ideas

*Market research and channel identity for Krymzia, plus a ranked idea bank.*

## 1. Niche analysis

### Audience demand
"Independent music production / artist-development" content has durable, evergreen demand: millions of hobbyist musicians and bedroom producers search YouTube for both inspiration ("how indie artists actually make it") and instruction ("how to mix vocals at home"). Demand doesn't spike-and-die like trend/news niches — a good process video keeps getting search traffic for years.

### Competition landscape
Three existing lanes, each with a gap Krymzia can sit between:
1. **Faceless tutorial channels** (mixing/production tips, no artist identity, no real songs) — high volume, low personality, easy to out-differentiate on personality/story.
2. **Polished major/label-adjacent artist channels** — high production value, zero process transparency, unreachable budget to compete with directly.
3. **"Day in the life of an indie artist" vloggers** — strong parasocial pull, often weak on actual musical craft/output.

**Krymzia's gap:** craft + process + real songs + honest small-channel journey, in one identity. Small channels rarely combine all four; most pick one and stay shallow on the others.

### Search potential
Target long-tail, high-intent keywords rather than competing head-on for generic terms:
- High opportunity: "making a song from a voice memo," "how to release your first song," "bedroom producer to [X] plays," "mixing vocals with no expensive gear," "songwriting for beginners."
- Low opportunity (too competitive/generic for a new channel): "music production tutorial," "how to make beats," "songwriting tips" (dominated by large channels — use as inspiration, not head-to-head titles).

### Monetization opportunities
Music-artist-creator channels monetize unusually well relative to subscriber count once past YPP, because the audience is high-intent (fans, not just viewers) and there are music-specific revenue lines (streaming payouts, sync, licensing, merch, direct fan funding) layered on top of standard YouTube monetization. See file `06`.

### Content longevity
Process/tutorial content is evergreen (search traffic compounds for years). Song releases and reality/vlog content are time-bound but drive the parasocial connection that makes the evergreen content convert to subscribers. The channel needs both: evergreen for discovery, timely for connection.

### Differentiation angle
**"Show your work."** Every video — even a pure music video — should carry at least a trace of the process (a caption noting a production choice, a Community post about what almost didn't make the final cut). This single habit is the thread that makes a music channel and a process channel feel like *one* channel instead of two competing identities.

## 2. Channel identity

- **Name:** Krymzia (keep as-is — already has brand equity from other platforms/bio).
- **Tagline:** "Building a band in public — the songs and everything behind them."
- **Visual identity:** consistent color grade/thumbnail template across all three content types so the channel reads as one brand even when format varies (music video vs. talking-head vs. Short).
- **Voice:** honest, unpolished-but-competent, self-aware about being small — never faking bigger production than it has. Audiences in this niche reward authenticity over polish; a shaky-cam "here's the actual mess of my studio" video will often outperform an over-produced one.

## 3. Target viewer profile

**Primary persona — "Bedroom Producer Ben"**
- 16–30 years old, makes music alone or with 1–2 collaborators, has released 0–5 songs, watches YouTube for both inspiration and technique, is the exact person who will click "how someone with 300 subs actually wrote and released a song."
- Wants: proof it's possible, concrete technique, a channel to feel part of before it's big.

**Secondary persona — "Genuine Fan Fiona"**
- Found Krymzia through a song/short, doesn't care about production tips, wants the music, the story, and to feel early/in on something.
- Wants: songs, personality, a reason to check back and eventually pay (merch, memberships, concerts).

Design every video to serve at least one persona fully; the strongest videos serve both (a making-of video: Ben watches for technique, Fiona watches for the story and the payoff song).

## 4. Content categories

1. **Song Origins** — the story/process behind a specific track
2. **Craft & Technique** — reusable, searchable skill content
3. **Artist Reality** — the honest small-channel/indie-artist journey
4. **Music Output** — the finished songs and visuals
5. **Gear & Setup** — tools, budget setups, software
6. **Community & Reaction** — audience-driven, low-production-cost content

## How to use this list

- Pull from **Tier 1 first**, in any order that matches songs you're actually releasing that month.
- Every idea should be re-checked against the positioning statement in file `01` before filming.
- Retire or rewrite ideas that assume milestones you haven't hit yet (e.g., #90, #92) until they're true — don't fake numbers or status.
- Refill this bank every 4–6 weeks using file `07`'s "what worked" data: for every video that overperforms, generate 3 variations of it before moving to a new topic.
